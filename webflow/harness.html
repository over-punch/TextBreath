The paragraph breathes. Each line drifts a little wider then a little tighter, offset in phase from the line above it, so the whole block seems to inhale and exhale.

A traveling wave rolls down these lines on the weight axis, each line thickening and thinning a fraction of a second after the one above, like a tide moving through the type.

What "working" looks like: both paragraphs animate continuously and subtly after the fonts load. In the first, adjacent lines are visibly out of phase (one widening while its neighbour narrows). In the second, a weight ripple travels top-to-bottom. Resize the window — the text re-groups into new lines and keeps breathing. Open the console and run TextBreath.destroy(document.querySelector('[data-textbreath]')) to freeze the first paragraph, or TextBreath.restart() to re-init all. With prefers-reduced-motion enabled the text renders normally but does not animate.
